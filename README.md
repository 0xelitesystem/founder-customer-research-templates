# founder-customer-research-templates

Plain-text templates for talking to your customers and prospects. Discovery interviews, churn calls, surveys, jobs-to-be-done worksheets, win/loss debriefs. No SaaS, no signup, no analytics. Open the markdown, copy what you need.

## What's in here

| File | When to use |
|------|-------------|
| [`templates/01-discovery-interview.md`](templates/01-discovery-interview.md) | First call with a prospective customer. You don't know if there's a real problem yet. |
| [`templates/02-problem-validation-survey.md`](templates/02-problem-validation-survey.md) | You have a hypothesis about a problem. You want to test it across more people than you can interview. |
| [`templates/03-churn-exit-interview.md`](templates/03-churn-exit-interview.md) | A paying customer canceled. You have 20 minutes to learn what went wrong before they're gone. |
| [`templates/04-jtbd-switch-interview.md`](templates/04-jtbd-switch-interview.md) | A customer recently bought your product. Reconstruct the timeline that led to the purchase. |
| [`templates/05-feature-prioritization-survey.md`](templates/05-feature-prioritization-survey.md) | You have a backlog and limited engineering hours. You want to know which features your customers actually care about. |
| [`templates/06-win-loss-interview.md`](templates/06-win-loss-interview.md) | A deal closed or fell through. You want to know why. Run this with sales prospects, not existing customers. |
| [`templates/07-onboarding-followup.md`](templates/07-onboarding-followup.md) | A user signed up but hasn't returned. You want to understand the gap between expectation and first session. |
| [`templates/08-pricing-research.md`](templates/08-pricing-research.md) | You're setting or changing prices. You want signal beyond "what do you think a fair price is?" (which is a bad question). |

## How to actually run an interview

Most founders ask leading questions and hear what they want to hear. The templates push back on this:

- Past behavior, not future intentions. "When did you last try to solve this?" beats "Would you use a product that...?".
- Specific instances, not generalizations. "Tell me about the most recent time" beats "How often do you...?".
- Silence after a question. Count to five. People fill silence with their real answer.
- Don't pitch. The moment you describe what you're building, the interview is contaminated. Keep your product out of the first two-thirds of every call.
- Record the call (with permission) and re-listen. You missed half of what they said the first time.

## Format

Every template is a single markdown file with three sections:

1. **Goal of the call/survey** - what you should walk away with
2. **Script or question list** - what to ask, in order
3. **Anti-patterns** - questions that look useful but produce useless data

## Adapting the templates

These are starting points. Cut questions, reorder them, change the wording. The goal is to get to a real conversation as fast as possible, not to read a script.

If a question feels awkward in your context, it probably is. Replace it.
