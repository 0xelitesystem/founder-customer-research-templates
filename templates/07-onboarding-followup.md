# 07. Onboarding followup

## Goal

A user signed up. They did something (or nothing). They haven't come back. You have a small window to understand the gap between what they expected and what they got.

This template assumes self-serve signup. For sales-led onboarding, use template 06 instead.

## When to send

- Signed up 3-7 days ago
- Has not returned (or returned but did not complete a meaningful action)
- Did not request a refund or cancel (those are different conversations)

Past 14 days, they've fully moved on. They won't respond, and if they do, they don't remember enough to be useful.

## Outreach (email)

Subject: Quick question about signing up for [product]

Body:

> Hi [name],
>
> I'm [name], one of the people who works on [product]. I noticed you signed up [N days ago] but haven't been back since.
>
> I'm not writing to win you back. I'd really like to know what made you stop. Two questions:
>
> 1. What were you hoping [product] would help with?
> 2. What stopped you from sticking around?
>
> Even a one-sentence reply would be useful. Thanks.

Send from a real human address. Not "support@" or "no-reply@".

Response rate on a personal one-to-one email like this runs 10-25 percent. Mass automated drip campaigns asking the same questions run 1-3 percent.

## Why this email works

The line "I'm not writing to win you back" is critical. Without it, the recipient assumes any reply will trigger a sales sequence and stays silent.

The two-question structure produces shorter, more honest answers than open-ended "tell us about your experience". Specific beats vague.

## What to do with the replies

Build a simple spreadsheet:

| Column | Notes |
|--------|-------|
| Signup date | |
| Days to non-response | |
| Channel they came from | |
| Q1 answer (what they hoped) | The expected use case |
| Q2 answer (what stopped them) | The actual reason |
| Replied: yes/no | |

After 20-30 replies, you'll see a small number of repeating patterns:

- **"I couldn't figure out how to [thing]"**: onboarding UX problem.
- **"I needed [feature] and you don't have it"**: positioning problem (the marketing implied it, or you should genuinely build it).
- **"I just got distracted / forgot"**: activation timing problem. The first session needs to deliver value, not require a follow-up session.
- **"It wasn't quite what I expected"**: positioning problem on the landing page. Pull the verbatim quote and look at your homepage with their words in mind.

## When to follow up with a call

If a reply is short but the user matches your ideal customer profile, send one follow-up:

> "Thanks for replying. Would you be open to a 15-minute call this week or next? I'm trying to understand the [their phrase] problem better and your perspective would help. No pitch."

For users who reply with frustration or a specific feature gap, a call gives you 10x the data. For users who reply with "wasn't a fit for me", thank them and move on.

## Anti-patterns

| Don't | Why |
|-------|-----|
| Use an automated drip "we noticed you haven't logged in!" sequence | Trains users to ignore your emails. Tiny response rate. No qualitative data. |
| Offer a discount or extension to get a response | Now you're paying for replies and you can't tell honest answers from incentive-driven ones. |
| Send from a fake-personal address (e.g., a CEO name but actually a marketing tool) | If they reply and get a generic auto-response, trust is gone. Send from a real inbox you check. |
| Ignore users who came from paid channels | Their reasons for churning are often the most useful (the ad set the wrong expectation). |
| Ask 8 questions | Send 2. Maybe 3. They will answer 2 honestly and 8 not at all. |

## After 90 days

Once you've collected 50+ responses, the patterns are no longer surprising. At that point, stop sending the same email and start solving the top 2-3 reasons. Re-run the survey 6 months later if you want to verify the fixes worked.
