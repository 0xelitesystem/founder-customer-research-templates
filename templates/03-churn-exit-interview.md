# 03. Churn exit interview

## Goal

A paying customer just canceled or downgraded. Find out the real reason in 15-20 minutes before they disappear.

The reason in the cancellation form is almost never the full story. "Budget cuts" usually means "you weren't worth the money". "We're using something else" usually means "your competitor solved the part we cared about". You're trying to get past the surface-level answer.

## When to send the request

- Self-serve cancellation: within 24 hours
- Customer-success-managed account: within 48 hours
- Annual contract not renewed: within 1 week of the renewal lapse

Past 2 weeks, recall fades fast and they've moved on. The signal degrades.

## Setup

> "Thanks for taking 15 minutes. I'm [name], one of the people who works on [product]. I'm not going to try to sell you back, and I'm not going to argue with anything you say. I just want to understand what happened so we can do better. The honest stuff is more useful to me than the polite stuff."

Mean it. The moment you push back on a complaint, the call is over. Listen.

## Script

### Part 1: Context (2-3 minutes)

1. How long were you using [product]?
2. What did you originally sign up to do with it?
3. Who else on your team used it?

### Part 2: The decision (5-7 minutes, the core)

4. When did you first start thinking about canceling? What triggered that?

This is the question. Listen carefully. Don't interrupt. Don't pitch.

5. Between then and when you actually canceled, what happened? Did anything almost change your mind?
6. Who made the final call? Was it just you, or was there a decision-making process?
7. What's the timeline of an average day or week now, where you would have used [product]?
8. (If they switched) What are you using instead? How did you find it?

### Part 3: The product itself (5 minutes)

9. What did [product] do well for you?
10. What was the biggest thing that didn't work?
11. Was there a moment where you thought "this isn't going to work for us"? When was it?
12. Did you reach out to support or sales about any of this? What happened?

### Part 4: The alternative (3-5 minutes, if they switched)

13. What does [new tool] do that we didn't?
14. What do you miss about [our product], if anything?
15. How is the new tool priced? Did price factor into the switch?

### Wrap-up

16. If we changed one thing, what would have made you stay?
17. Would it be okay to follow up in 6 months and ask how things are going?

## Anti-patterns

| Don't | Why |
|-------|-----|
| Offer a discount mid-call | Tells them their reasons are negotiable. Kills honesty. Save retention offers for a separate call. |
| Defend the product | "Actually, that feature does exist" ends the interview. Note it, fix the discoverability later. |
| Ask "What feature would have kept you?" early | They'll invent something to be helpful. Use Q16 at the end after context is established. |
| Have a salesperson run the call | Their incentive is misaligned. Use a PM or founder. |
| Skip the call for low-value customers | The 200 dollar-a-month accounts churning for the same reason are killing your business. Talk to them. |

## What to record

After every churn call, log these in a spreadsheet or simple doc:

| Field | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| Customer name and plan | |
| Days as customer | |
| Reason given on cancellation form | Compare to the actual reason from the call |
| Triggering event (Q4 answer) | Verbatim if possible |
| Alternative they switched to | |
| The "one thing" (Q16) | |
| Direct quote that stuck with you | One sentence |

Re-read the log every quarter. Look for repeats. Three customers giving the same Q16 answer is a roadmap item.

## When you should NOT do this

- Customer is angry and venting on social media. Resolve the immediate complaint first. Schedule the interview for two weeks later.
- The cancellation is involuntary (failed payment, expired card). That's a billing issue, not churn signal. Email instead.
- They've already given detailed written feedback. Reading their email and acknowledging it beats forcing another call.
