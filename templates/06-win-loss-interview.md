# 06. Win/loss interview

## Goal

A sales conversation just ended in a yes or a no. You want to know why. The sales team has a story, the prospect has a story, and they're rarely the same story.

This template separates wins and losses because the questions differ.

## Who runs the call

NOT the salesperson who worked the deal. The prospect won't tell them the truth - either because they don't want to hurt feelings (won deals) or because they want to avoid an argument (lost deals).

Best: a founder, a PMM, a customer-success person, or a third-party researcher.

## Timing

- Closed-won: 2-4 weeks after they sign. Past 4 weeks, the buying memory fades.
- Closed-lost: 1-3 weeks after the no. Past 3 weeks, you're forgotten and they won't take the call.

## Setup (won deals)

> "Thanks for taking the call. I'm not part of the sales team; I work on understanding why customers like [company] choose us. The questions are about the buying process, not the product itself. Honest answers help us, even the unflattering ones."

## Setup (lost deals)

> "Thanks for hopping on. I know we didn't end up working together and I'm not here to change your mind. I'm trying to understand the process from your side so we don't waste your time or ours next time. 15 minutes, no pitch."

## Won-deal script

### Part 1: How they found you

1. When you first started looking for a [category] tool, where did you start?
2. How did you find [company]?
3. What other tools were on your shortlist?

### Part 2: Evaluation

4. What did you do to evaluate [company]? Demo, trial, references?
5. Who else was involved in the decision? What did they care about?
6. Was there a moment in the evaluation where you thought "this is the one"? When?
7. Was there anything that almost made you pick someone else?

### Part 3: The decision

8. What was the final reason you chose [company]?
9. How was the price discussion? Did you negotiate?
10. How fast did the contract process feel? Was there anything that slowed it down?

### Part 4: Looking back (30 days in)

11. Has the product met what you expected during the sales process? Where has it not?
12. If a friend at another company asked, "should I look at [company]?", what would you say?
13. What part of the sales process should we do differently next time?

## Lost-deal script

### Part 1: Why they were looking

1. What problem were you originally trying to solve when you came to us?
2. Was there a deadline or trigger event behind the search?

### Part 2: How we did

3. How did you find [company] in the first place?
4. What was your impression after the first conversation?
5. What was missing for you?

### Part 3: Who won

6. Who did you go with? (Some won't say. Don't push.)
7. What did they have that we didn't?
8. Was it price, features, fit, timing, or relationship?

The five categories matter. Most losses are categorized as "price" in CRMs, but real reasons are:

- **Price**: budget genuinely too high.
- **Feature**: we were missing something specific they needed.
- **Fit**: we serve a different segment than they actually were.
- **Timing**: they aren't ready (and probably won't be in 6 months either).
- **Relationship**: a competitor had a contact, a champion, or a track record we didn't.

Get them to tell you which one. The right action for each is different.

### Part 4: What we could have done

9. Was there a point in the process where, if we had done something differently, we'd have won the deal?
10. Did anything we said make you less interested? Be brutal here, it's the most useful answer.
11. (If they say "send something different") What would have changed your mind?

### Wrap-up

12. Would it be okay to reach out in 6-12 months to see how things are going?

## Anti-patterns

| Don't | Why |
|-------|-----|
| Have sales run the interview | They're not getting the truth. The data is contaminated. |
| Ask "What features were you missing?" | Leading. Frames the loss as a feature gap when it might be fit or trust. |
| Pitch at the end of a lost-deal call | Confirms their decision was right. Don't. |
| Skip lost-deal calls because "they don't matter anymore" | The pattern across lost deals is your most important pipeline data. |
| Trust the CRM "lost reason" field | It's the sales rep's interpretation, not the buyer's. Useful for trend tracking, not for understanding individual deals. |

## After 10-15 interviews

Look for patterns:

- **In won deals**: what's the most common "moment they decided"? That's the part of the sales process you should invest in more.
- **In lost deals**: what's the most common real reason? If it's "price", you have a positioning or qualification problem (not always a pricing problem - you may be reaching prospects who can't afford you).
- **In both**: who's NOT on the call that should be? Procurement, security, IT, executive sponsor? Their absence in your process explains many losses.
