# 04. JTBD switch interview

## Goal

You have a recent customer (signed up in the last 30-90 days). Reconstruct the timeline that led to their purchase: what was happening in their life or work, when did they decide they needed a solution, why they chose you over alternatives.

This is the "switch" interview from the Jobs-to-be-Done framework. The output tells you:
- The actual triggers that cause buying (these go in your marketing)
- The forces that push and pull people toward and away from your product
- Which alternatives you actually compete with (often surprising)

## Who to interview

- They bought in the last 30-90 days. Memory is fresh.
- They paid real money. Free trial users haven't truly committed.
- A real human made the decision. For B2B, that's the person who signed the contract or filed the expense report.

Skip anyone who signed up impulsively for less than $20. The signal is weaker because the bar was lower.

## Setup

> "Thanks for taking the call. I'm trying to understand the story of how you ended up using [product]. I'm going to walk you through your timeline starting from before you knew about us. Some questions might feel like ancient history. The details matter."

## Script

The script has four time markers. You're walking backward from "now" to "before you'd thought about a solution".

### Marker 1: First thought (5 minutes)

1. Let's start at the beginning. When was the first time you remember thinking "I need to solve this problem"?
2. What was happening that day or that week? What triggered the thought?
3. Was it sudden, or had it been building?

Listen for the trigger. It's almost always a specific event, not a slow realization. A failed quarterly report. A complaint from a teammate. A subscription renewing that they didn't want to pay for.

### Marker 2: Active looking (5-7 minutes)

4. Once you decided to look for a solution, what did you do first?
5. Did you search online? Ask someone? Look at an existing tool?
6. What did you search for? Try to remember the exact words.
7. What were the first three options you found?
8. How did you narrow it down?

The search query is gold. It tells you the words your customers actually use, which is rarely the same as the words you use on your homepage.

### Marker 3: Comparison (5-7 minutes)

9. Which options did you seriously consider? List them.
10. What were the tradeoffs?
11. Was there anything that almost made you go with [competitor]? What was it?
12. Was there anything that almost made you not buy anything? What pushed back against making a decision?

Question 12 is critical. The forces against switching are:
- **Anxiety about the new thing**: "Will this be hard to learn?"
- **Habit with the current thing**: "We already have a process for this even if it sucks."

If your product loses customers to "do nothing", these are the forces you have to defeat. Often more important than competing on features.

### Marker 4: Decision (3-5 minutes)

13. What was the final thing that made you choose [product]?
14. Who else was involved in the decision? How did you convince them?
15. Was it a single decision, or did you trial first?
16. How did you justify the cost? Was that hard?

### Wrap-up

17. Looking back, what made the difference between "I should solve this" and "I'm actually solving this"?
18. Knowing what you know now, would you start the search differently?

## What the JTBD frame is doing

You're mapping four forces:

```
        PUSH (current situation is bad)
              |
              v
   [staying]  ->  [switching]
              ^
              |
        PULL (new solution looks good)

   ANXIETY (new thing scary)  <- opposing the switch
   HABIT  (current thing comfortable) <- opposing the switch
```

For a switch to happen, **push + pull > anxiety + habit**.

Most marketing only emphasizes pull ("look how great our product is"). The interview surfaces the push (which the customer rarely articulates without prompting) and the anxiety/habit forces you need to neutralize.

## Anti-patterns

| Don't | Why |
|-------|-----|
| Ask "Why did you choose us?" as the first question | They'll give a rationalized post-hoc answer. Walk backward instead. |
| Skip Marker 1 | The trigger is the whole story. Without it, you have no "why now". |
| Let them generalize about "people like me" | You want their specific story. "Tell me about the day you decided." |
| Stop at the decision | Marker 4 is half the answer. Why-not-buy is as important as why-buy. |

## After 5-10 of these interviews

Pattern match across the calls:

1. **Triggers**: What specific events do customers experience right before they look?
2. **Search queries**: What words do they actually type?
3. **Competition**: Who do they compare you to? (Often "spreadsheets" and "doing nothing" are top 2.)
4. **Anxiety**: What worries do they share? Onboarding effort? Vendor lock-in? Trust?
5. **Deciding line**: What was the one fact or feature that closed the deal?

Each of these patterns is a marketing or product action item.
